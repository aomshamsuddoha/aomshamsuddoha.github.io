About

Profile

I am a Lecturer in Electrical and Electronic Engineering at the University of Liberal Arts Bangladesh (ULAB) in Dhaka, where I have also served as Program Coordinator of the B.Sc. in EEE program since February 2025. Over nearly six years I have taught VLSI, solid-state devices, electronic circuits, power electronics and embedded systems at four universities.

My research began with my undergraduate thesis on low-power SRAM design using FinFETs. That work became my first-author paper, which combines adiabatic logic with FinFET-based SRAM to reduce power consumption. Since then I have co-authored seven peer-reviewed IEEE papers spanning circuit design, wireless communication, and machine learning for healthcare, often while mentoring undergraduate research teams.

I now want to commit to hardware research full time. I am looking for a PhD position in Electrical and Computer Engineering where I can work on low-power VLSI, in-memory computing and new transistor architectures. I bring hands-on experience with Cadence Virtuoso, Verilog and PSpice, and years of practice explaining circuits clearly.

Research

Research interests

Primary: hardware and computingIn order of priority

  1. Low-power VLSI

    Circuit and device techniques that cut dynamic and leakage power in digital logic and memory, including FinFET-based design and adiabatic (energy-recovery) logic.

  2. In-memory computing

    Performing computation inside or beside memory arrays such as SRAM, to reduce the energy and delay spent moving data between memory and processor.

  3. Novel transistor architectures and semiconductor devices

    FinFET, gate-all-around and other emerging devices, and how their electrical behaviour shapes circuit and memory design.

  4. Beyond von Neumann computer architecture

    Architectures that loosen the separation between processing and memory to overcome the memory-wall bottleneck.

  5. Neuromorphic computing

    Brain-inspired hardware that uses spiking or analog computation for efficient learning and inference.

Applications

  1. Biomedical engineering

    Signal and image analysis for healthcare, building on my co-authored work on EEG-based seizure detection and brain tumour classification in MRI.

  2. Wearable electronics

    Body-worn sensing systems, where circuit-level energy efficiency directly sets battery life and usability.

Featured publication

First-author · Based on my B.Sc. thesis

Design and Performance Analysis of SRAM Circuit Using Adiabatic Logic with FinFET

A. O. M. Shamsuddoha, M. K. Islam and S. N. Biswas

2021 International Conference on Electronics, Communications and Information Technology (ICECIT), Bangladesh, pp. 1–4

This paper applies adiabatic (energy-recovery) logic to an SRAM cell built with FinFET devices and analyses the resulting power and performance. It grew out of my undergraduate thesis, supervised by Prof. Satyendra Nath Biswas, and it is the work most closely aligned with the research I want to pursue in my PhD.
